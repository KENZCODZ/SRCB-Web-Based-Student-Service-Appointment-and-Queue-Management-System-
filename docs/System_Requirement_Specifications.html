System Requirement Specifications (SRS)

EduQueue: Web-Based Student Service Appointment and Queue Management System

Group 2 | Capstone & Web Systems Project | S.Y. 2025–2026
St. Rita's College of Balingasag, Inc. • Balingasag, Misamis Oriental

1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document specifies the comprehensive functional and non-functional requirements for the EduQueue: Web-Based Student Service Appointment and Queue Management System. The primary objective of this SRS is to establish a rigorous, formal specification of the software solution developed for St. Rita's College of Balingasag (SRCB). It serves as the definitive reference agreement between the system developers, academic evaluators, institutional administrators, department heads (Registrar, Dean's Office, Guidance Office, Finance/Cashier, IT Helpdesk, and Library), and the student body.

The system replaces manual paper logs, congested physical walk-in queues, and fragmented scheduling with a centralized, full-stack digital platform featuring real-time walk-in sequence ticketing, conflict-free appointment slot booking, role-based staff desk calling terminals, a lobby display monitor, and automated transactional email notifications.

1.2 Project Scope

The EduQueue system is an enterprise-grade, full-stack web application architected with a React 18 + TypeScript + Vite frontend, a native PHP 8.2+ RESTful API backend adhering to the MVC and Service Layer architecture, and a normalized MySQL 8.0 relational database.

The scope encompasses:

  • Student Self-Service Portal: Self-registration, JWT credential authentication, interactive service catalog discovery, conflict-free time slot appointment scheduling, on-demand walk-in queue ticketing, personal real-time position tracking, in-app notification center, and profile management.
  • Staff Operations & Counter Desk Terminal: Calling next student, starting service, completing transactions, skipping no-shows, and processing appointment dispositions (Approval with email voucher, Decline with reason, and Reschedule with revised date/time).
  • Public Lobby Live Display Monitor (/display): Fullscreen lobby kiosk display featuring live called tickets per counter desk, active ticket callouts, real-time digital clock, and upcoming waiting lists with auto-refresh polling.
  • Administrative Management Panel: Operational KPI overview, 7-day volume analytics, service & operating hours configuration, user account provisioning, system audit logging, and transactional email dispatch logs.
  • Multi-Channel Transactional Email Engine: Seamless integration with Resend, Brevo, SendGrid, SMTP, and Log driver for automated delivery of appointment confirmations, cancellations, and reschedule advisories.

2. Overall Description

2.1 User Needs and Pain Points

  • Need 1: Eliminating Physical Queue Congestion: During peak enrollment and clearance periods, students endure hours standing in crowded hallways outside school offices without knowing their queue status or estimated wait duration.
  • Need 2: Conflict-Free Appointment Scheduling: Services requiring extensive face-to-face consultation (e.g., Dean's Academic Advising, Guidance Counseling, Transcript evaluation) suffer from double bookings, uncoordinated schedules, and lost manual appointment logbooks.
  • Need 3: Real-Time Transparency for Staff and Campus Lobby: Office staff have no visibility into daily student load, cannot forecast incoming student volume, and lack digital calling screens in lobby areas, causing missed turns and chaotic counter crowds.
  • Need 4: Automated Verification & Communication: When appointment dates change or cancellations occur, staff have no automated medium to notify students promptly, resulting in wasted trips to campus.
  • Need 5: Centralized Institutional Auditing & Capacity Control: Department heads and school administrators lack unified reporting tools to evaluate office turnaround times, monitor staff activity, and adjust daily student quotas.

2.2 User Personas

Persona 1 – The College Student: "Juan Dela Cruz"

Demographics: 20 years old, 3rd Year BS Information Technology, Working Student.

Goals: Book Transcript of Records requests around his class timetable; join cashier queues virtually without missing lectures; receive SMS/email alerts when his ticket is called.

Frustrations: Long physical lines at the Registrar and Cashier offices; unclear document requirements resulting in repeated office visits; lack of real-time queue position tracking.

Persona 2 – The Department Staff: "Maria Santos"

Demographics: 34 years old, Administrative Staff, Registrar Office.

Goals: View daily student appointments in advance; call next walk-in ticket with one click; communicate appointment approvals, reschedules, or rejections with automated email receipts.

Frustrations: Disorganized physical crowds around her counter window; manual logbooks and paper clearance forms; handling frustrated students who missed their turn.

Persona 3 – The System Administrator & Dean: "Dr. Ernesto Ramos"

Demographics: 48 years old, Dean / Academic Administrator.

Goals: Configure office operating hours and daily capacity thresholds; provision staff accounts; inspect system-wide audit logs and email delivery metrics; evaluate peak hour traffic analytics.

Frustrations: Absence of centralized operational reports; inability to restrict daily service volume to prevent staff burnout; unorganized student clearance tracking.

2.3 Assumptions and Dependencies

  • Users have access to modern web browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge) with JavaScript enabled.
  • The application is a full-stack system comprising a React frontend (Vite + TypeScript) and native PHP REST API backend connected to MySQL 8.0.
  • Stateless authentication is enforced via JWT (HMAC-SHA256). Public lobby screens (/display) do not require authentication.
  • Transactional email delivery utilizes configured mail API keys (Resend, Brevo, SendGrid) or SMTP fallback, with automatic fail-safe logging into the MySQL database.
  • Development execution is supported via a unified single-command runner (npm run dev) executing both backend and frontend processes.

3. System Features and Requirements

3.1 Student Self-Service & Authentication

  • FR1 The system shall provide a registration form allowing students to register with their Student ID Number, First Name, Last Name, institutional Email, Password, Contact Number, Degree Program, and Year Level.
  • FR2 The system shall enforce client-side and server-side validation to ensure Student ID Numbers and Email addresses are unique within the database.
  • FR3 The system shall hash user passwords using standard PASSWORD_BCRYPT with a cost factor of 10 prior to database insertion.
  • FR4 The system shall authenticate users via email or student ID number along with password, issuing an HMAC-SHA256 JWT upon successful verification.
  • FR5 The system shall enforce Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) redirecting students to /dashboard, staff to /admin/appointments, and administrators to /admin/dashboard.
  • FR6 The system shall provide quick one-click demo login buttons on the login screen for rapid evaluative testing of Admin, Staff, and Student roles.
  • FR7 The system shall allow authenticated users to update personal profile details and change account passwords with current password verification.

3.2 Institutional Service Catalog

  • FR8 The system shall display a service catalog categorized by office (Registrar, Dean's Office, Guidance, Finance/Cashier, IT Helpdesk, Library).
  • FR9 Each service card shall indicate its service classification (appointment or queue), office counter location, estimated duration in minutes, daily quota, and prerequisites/requirements.
  • FR10 The system shall provide dynamic search and category filtering allowing students to filter services instantly without reloading the page.

3.3 Appointment Booking & Conflict Management

  • FR11 The system shall compute and display available appointment time slots for any selected date based on service operating schedules, slot duration, and maximum capacity limits.
  • FR12 The system shall prevent overlapping bookings by checking existing active appointments for the requested time window before booking confirmation.
  • FR13 The system shall prevent students from booking past dates, weekend non-operating days, or slots exceeding maximum daily capacity.
  • FR14 The system shall assign a unique tracking code (e.g., APT-20260904-XXXX) to each confirmed appointment.
  • FR15 The system shall allow students to cancel pending appointments, automatically freeing up the time slot for other students.

3.4 Walk-in Queue Ticketing & Real-Time Position Tracking

  • FR16 The system shall allow registered students to join the walk-in queue for an active queue-based service with a single click.
  • FR17 The system shall automatically generate sequential daily queue ticket numbers formatted with service code prefixes (e.g., FIN-001, REG-002, IT-003).
  • FR18 The system shall restrict a student to one active queue ticket per service at any given time to prevent queue hoarding.
  • FR19 The system shall calculate and display the student's real-time queue position, the ticket currently being served at the counter, and estimated remaining wait time.
  • FR20 The system shall provide a visual status badge (waiting, calling, serving, completed, skipped, cancelled) that updates in real time.

3.5 Staff Appointment Processing & Email Dispatch

  • FR21 The system shall provide staff with an Appointment Management dashboard filtered by service, status, date range, and student search query.
  • FR22 The system shall allow staff to Approve pending appointments, transitioning status to approved and triggering an automated HTML email voucher to the student.
  • FR23 The system shall allow staff to Decline appointments with mandatory reason input, dispatching an official cancellation advisory email.
  • FR24 The system shall allow staff to Reschedule appointments with revised date, start time, end time, and staff notes, notifying the student via email.
  • FR25 The system shall log every email dispatch attempt into the email_logs database table.

3.6 Staff Queue Counter Desk Terminal

  • FR26 The system shall provide staff with a Counter Calling Terminal showing the current queue list for their assigned service desk.
  • FR27 The system shall provide a prominent "Call Next Student" action that selects the oldest waiting ticket, marks it as calling, assigns the staff counter window, and broadcasts to the public display.
  • FR28 The system shall allow staff to transition ticket states to "Start Serving" (serving) upon student arrival at the counter.
  • FR29 The system shall allow staff to finalize transactions by clicking "Complete" (completed) or mark no-shows with "Skip" (skipped).
  • FR30 The system shall track timestamps for called_at, served_at, and completed_at to compute accurate service turnaround analytics.

3.7 Public Lobby Live Display Monitor (/display)

  • FR31 The system shall provide a dedicated public route at /display accessible without user authentication.
  • FR32 The display shall render high-contrast visual cards for every active counter desk indicating the service name, counter window, and currently called ticket number.
  • FR33 The display shall feature an upcoming waiting list ticker, a live digital clock, and institutional SRCB branding with auto-refresh polling every 5 seconds.

3.8 Administrator Control Panel, Services & Audit Logs

  • FR34 The dashboard shall display live summary KPI cards: Total Appointments Today, Active Walk-In Queue Count, Completed Services, and Total Registered Users.
  • FR35 The dashboard shall render a 7-Day Throughput Bar Chart comparing appointment volume against walk-in queue transactions.
  • FR36 The system shall allow administrators to create and edit service records including code, title, category, type, counter location, duration, daily capacity, and prerequisites.
  • FR37 The system shall allow administrators to configure recurring weekly operating schedules with custom slot durations and capacity limits per time slot.
  • FR38 The system shall allow administrators to provision new Staff or Administrator accounts directly with assigned departments and toggle user status.
  • FR39 The system shall log all critical actions into an immutable audit_logs database table with user ID, action tag, entity type, IP address, and JSON metadata.
  • FR40 The system shall provide a dedicated email dispatch monitor detailing every outbound notification and delivery status.

4. Non-Functional Requirements (HCI Focus)

4.1 Usability

  • NF1 A user shall be able to reach any major functional section within a maximum of 3 clicks from the main navigation menu.
  • NF2 The primary task of discovering a service, selecting an available time slot, entering purpose, and submitting an appointment booking shall be completable in under 60 seconds.
  • NF3 All interactive forms shall provide clear, real-time inline validation with descriptive error feedback before network dispatch.
  • NF4 The interface shall utilize a polished institutional color palette (SRCB Navy #1E3A8A, Royal Blue #2563EB, Emerald Green #10B981, Amber #F59E0B) with consistent typography and micro-animations.
  • NF5 All operational adjustments (service hours, counter names, slot capacities) shall be performed entirely through graphical UI modals without requiring manual SQL.

4.2 Performance

  • NF6 Backend REST API endpoints shall execute and respond within 200 milliseconds under standard institutional network loads.
  • NF7 Initial page loads shall render within 1.5 seconds on modern desktop browsers and under 3 seconds on 4G mobile connections.
  • NF8 All frequently queried columns (user_id, service_id, appointment_date, queue_date, status) shall be indexed in MySQL for sub-10ms query execution.
  • NF9 Live queue trackers and notification centers shall utilize lightweight, debounced REST polling (5-second intervals).

4.3 Accessibility

  • NF10 The application shall adhere to WCAG 2.1 Level AA accessibility criteria across all screens.
  • NF11 All body typography, headings, buttons, and alert badges shall maintain a minimum color contrast ratio of 4.5:1 against their backgrounds.
  • NF12 All interactive elements shall be fully navigable and actionable using only keyboard inputs (Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, Space, Escape).
  • NF13 All custom dialogs and icons shall incorporate appropriate ARIA attributes for screen reader compatibility.

4.4 Compatibility & Responsiveness

  • NF14 The web application shall render identically on latest versions of Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge, and mobile browser variants.
  • NF15 The UI shall dynamically adapt across viewports from 320px (mobile) to 1920px+ (large monitor / Lobby TV Kiosk) using Tailwind CSS responsive utility classes.
  • NF16 All buttons and interactive controls shall provide minimum touch target dimensions of 44x44 pixels for touchscreen mobile navigation.

4.5 Security

  • NF17 All database interactions shall strictly utilize PDO prepared statements with parameterized inputs. Emulated prepared statements shall be disabled.
  • NF18 All user credentials shall be secured using standard PASSWORD_BCRYPT with cost factor 10.
  • NF19 All private API routes shall validate incoming HMAC-SHA256 JWT tokens with standard 24-hour expiration claims.
  • NF20 Administrative and staff endpoints shall be guarded on the server side by RoleMiddleware.
  • NF21 All incoming request payloads shall be sanitized and escaped upon output formatting.
  • NF22 The backend API shall enforce explicit CORS origin headers and secure HTTP headers.
  • NF23 All secrets (database passwords, JWT keys, mail API credentials) shall reside exclusively in server-side .env files.

4.6 Maintainability & Architecture

  • NF24 The backend codebase shall strictly enforce separation of concerns across Core/, Middleware/, Controllers/, and Services/ layers.
  • NF25 The frontend application shall channel all network interactions through a centralized typed API service module (src/services/api.ts).
  • NF26 External email dispatch failures shall be captured gracefully and logged in email_logs without rolling back primary appointment or queue database transactions.

5. User Interface Design & Aesthetics

5.1 Wireframes & Layout Hierarchy

The user interface is structured into four distinct layout archetypes:

  1. Public & Guest Layout (/login, /register, /display): Clean, centered card authentication forms with institutional crest, quick demo autofill selectors, and clear status notifications; dedicated fullscreen lobby display layout with high-contrast typography and digital clock header.
  2. Student Self-Service Portal (/dashboard, /services, /appointments, /queue, /profile): Top navigation bar featuring brand identity, breadcrumbs, notification bell badge with live counter, user profile dropdown, active queue countdown ticker, and upcoming appointment alert cards.
  3. Staff Operations Terminal (/admin/appointments, /admin/queue): Streamlined calling interface with prominent status toggle buttons ("Call Next", "Start Serving", "Complete", "Skip") and filterable data tables with status color indicators.
  4. Administrator Control Panel (/admin/dashboard, /admin/services, /admin/users, /admin/audit-logs): Sidebar navigation for rapid switching between Dashboard, Services, Appointments, Queue Management, Users, and Audit Trails with analytical metric cards and SVG volume trend charts.

5.2 High-Fidelity UI Design System

  • Typography: Google Fonts Inter and Roboto for high-density tabular data and mobile legibility; Outfit for modern headings.
  • Color Palette: Deep Navy (#1E3A8A / #1E293B), Royal Blue (#2563EB), Emerald Green (#10B981), Warm Amber (#F59E0B), Rose Red (#EF4444), and Slate (#64748B / #F8FAFC).
  • Micro-Interactions: Subtle card hover elevations, glassmorphic modal overlays, skeleton loading cards, and semantic Lucide React icons pairing visual meaning with every interaction.
